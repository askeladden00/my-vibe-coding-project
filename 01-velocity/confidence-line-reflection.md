# Confidence Line Reflection (optional)

> Module 1 · Velocity. Toy vs tool, and where this build moved you on the Confidence Line.

## Toy or tool?

_A toy generates applause; a tool generates a decision. Which did you build, and how do you know?_

v1 was a toy. It looked clean, but partners only took away "a list of risk with numbers" and couldn't say what to do with it. v2 moved toward a tool: each account now has a recommended next step and a deadline, and a CSM can filter to high-risk accounts and open one to see why it is at risk. My round 2 partner said it felt like a real product because it was interactive and the numbers had a reference point.

It is not a tool yet. The partner would not show it to a VP: it is low fidelity, the flow isn't right, and the interactivity doesn't go deep enough. The data is also made up, so no real CSM could make a decision from it today.

## Confidence Line placement

_Where on the Confidence Line did Module 1 leave you, and why?_

Early on the line: I have a clickable prototype that people understand without me explaining it, but nothing is validated. Two rounds of Show & Swap showed that the screen makes sense once it has context, a health score benchmark and next steps. They did not show that CSMs would use it, that the churn predictions are accurate, or that the next steps would actually save accounts. Moving further needs a clear hypothesis to test (Module 2), real account data, and feedback from actual CSMs.

## The feedback that landed hardest

_The single piece of Show & Swap feedback that will change how you build next._

"With the data, and then so what? How do we act on the top risks?" I had built a screen that showed information but didn't help anyone act on it. From now on I'll start each screen from the decision the user needs to make and the action they take next, and only then decide which data to show.
